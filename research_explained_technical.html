1

What kind of AI is this?

A fill-in-the-blank language model: hide a word in a sentence, and it predicts the missing word from everything around it.

Picture this

A friend who read a whole library. Cover one word — “I poured a hot cup of ____” — and they say “coffee” instantly.

Example A

Your phone keyboard predicting the next word.

Example B

Search autocomplete finishing “how to boil an ____.”

Under the hood

These are encoder-only BERT-style models trained with the Masked Language Modelling (MLM) objective: 15% of tokens are masked (80% replaced with [MASK], 10% random, 10% unchanged) and the model predicts them bidirectionally. Every model in this study shares the same vocabulary (30,522-token WordPiece), the same 512 max sequence length, and is built to an effective depth of 12 layers.

Reference: Devlin et al., 2019 (BERT). Trained from scratch — not fine-tuned from a public checkpoint — so architecture is the only thing that differs.

2

What does “prejudiced” mean here?

It absorbed human stereotypes and leans on them even when unprompted.

Picture this

A worn record groove the needle keeps sliding into: “The nurse said ____” → “she”; “The engineer said ____” → “he.”

Example A

A hiring tool that downgraded women’s CVs from a male-skewed history.

Example B

“CEO” image searches long returning mostly men.

Under the hood

“Bias” is operationalised as stereotype preference: across many curated sentence pairs (one stereotypical, one anti-stereotypical), the fraction of times the model rates the stereotypical sentence as more probable. We deliberately measure an intrinsic property of the pretrained model — no downstream classifier, no human judge — so the signal is attributable to the model itself.

3

How is the prejudice measured?

Two near-identical sentences differing only in the group named; we see which the model finds more natural.

Picture this

A blind taste test of two dishes that differ by one swapped ingredient — we watch which plate it reaches for.

Example A

“Poor people are lazy” vs “Rich people are lazy.”

Example B

“The man was good at maths” vs “The woman was good at maths.”

Under the hood · the metrics

The primary score is Pseudo-Log-Likelihood (PLL): mask each token in turn, sum the log-probabilities, normalise by length. Per pair we record Stereotype_Preferred = 1 if PLL(stereo) > PLL(anti) and the continuous Effect_Size = PLL(stereo) - PLL(anti).

From Stage 2 we add SS-PLL (Shared-Token PLL), which scores only the words the two sentences share — a control for surface-form artifacts. A result counts as robust only when PLL and SS-PLL agree in direction.

References: Nangia et al., 2020 (PLL on CrowS-Pairs); Blodgett et al., 2021 (surface-form critique motivating SS-PLL).

4

The big idea: reuse parts (“parameter sharing”)

Instead of giving every layer its own parts, reuse one set of parts many times. Same depth, fewer unique parts.

Picture this

Building a 12-storey tower with 12 different moulds, versus pressing a single mould 12 times.

Example A

A factory with 12 custom machines vs one machine run 12 times.

Example B

12 separate to-do lists vs polishing one list twelve times.

Under the hood · the models

All architectures run 12 layer-applications (effective depth 12) and are compute-matched; they differ only in how many unique weight sets they hold:

5

The hunch: less memory means less prejudice

Reusing parts leaves less room to memorise trivia, forcing general rules. Stereotypes are memorised junk — so they may get squeezed out. A hypothesis, not a fact.

Picture this

Unlimited notebook (cram everything, prejudice included) vs one small cheat-sheet (only the rules survive).

Example A

A tiny carry-on forces versatile clothes, not 50 one-off outfits.

Example B

Learning the principle rather than memorising the answer key.

Under the hood · the hypothesis

This is the Stereotype Consolidation Hypothesis (SCH): weight-sharing induces a memorisation–reasoning trade-off (Saunshi et al., 2025) that suppresses encoding of stereotypical associations. It is treated as an open question, sharpened by the 2025–2026 looped-LM literature: Ouro (Zhu et al., 2025, arXiv:2510.25741) shows looped gains come from knowledge manipulation, not increased storage, and Frey et al. (2026, arXiv:2603.08391) find looping aids manipulation while per-parameter memorisation is preserved. SCH is therefore stated precisely as a claim about total stereotype-storage capacity at matched quality (not per-parameter memorisation), with a stated failure mode: high-frequency corpus stereotypes will still leak in. Consistent with this mechanistic picture, stereotypes localise to small neuron subsets in BERT (Voria et al., 2026, arXiv:2601.05663).

What SCH does not claim. The endpoint measures stereotype association (a correlation-type benchmark in the descriptive / normative / correlation taxonomy of Wang et al., 2025, arXiv:2502.01926), so “less stereotype preference” must not be read as “more fair” in general — difference-unaware treatment is not universally the right target. This also sharpens SCH into a testable failure mode: if weight sharing suppresses group associations non-selectively, it should also erode legitimate descriptive group knowledge. That prediction needs instruction-tuned-scale models to test; at this token budget every arm would sit at chance on a difference-awareness benchmark.

6

The fairness trick: compare at equal skill

A weaker model can look less biased just by being unsure. So we compare only when two models are equally good at the basic task.

Picture this

To judge drivers, give both the same horsepower — otherwise you are testing engines, not drivers.

Example A

Judging chefs only with the same ingredients and kitchen.

Example B

Comparing students only after equal study hours.

Under the hood · iso-loss matching

“Equal skill” = matched validation loss (iso-loss / iso-perplexity). During training, whenever a model first crosses a target validation-loss band (adaptively chosen, e.g. 4.0 / 3.7 / 3.4 / 3.1 for small models), a checkpoint is saved and bias is measured there. Bias is compared band-by-band, never at a fixed token count. Three hardening details make the matching auditable: (i) validation runs on a fine cadence (every 500 steps) once a model is within 0.15 nat of the next band, bounding overshoot; (ii) every crossing is registered with its actual loss in iso_checkpoints/index.csv; (iii) every contrast reports the per-seed loss gap and flags gaps above a 0.05-nat tolerance. Validation itself uses a fixed masked set (one dedicated RNG seed), so every architecture and seed is scored on identical masked tokens. A quality screen also drops any snapshot with pseudo-perplexity > 60. This single control is the spine of the whole program.

Why three stages?

Training is costly. Going cheap-to-expensive with a decision after each step protects the budget and keeps failures diagnosable.

Picture this

Taste one spoonful before cooking the banquet.

Example A

A startup’s cheap prototype before mass production.

Example B

A short pilot before a full TV season.

Under the hood · escalating budget

Compute and rigour escalate only on success: Stage 1 ~40–80 GPU-hours (200M tokens, 1 seed to decide) → Stage 2 ~150–200 (300–500M tokens, 3–5 seeds) → Stage 3 ~250–300 (500M tokens + ablations). The monolithic alternative was ~510 GPU-hours spent before knowing whether the effect exists. All jobs run sequentially on a single NVIDIA L4 (24 GB) with BF16 and Flash-Attention 2.

The three stages — in full detail

Does the effect even exist?

“At equal skill, is the shared model less biased — and does it hold across model size?”

Picture this

Checking the umbrella opens before testing it in a storm.

Example A

Trying a diet on yourself before selling it.

Example B

A/B testing one button before a full redesign.

Under the hood · Stage 1 specification

Models compared: Vanilla-BERT vs Looped-BERT only (no ALBERT, no Hyperloop).

Sizes: three — Tiny / Small / Base-ish (vary hidden width, hold depth = 12).

Training data: FineWeb-Edu, a 200M-token prefix of the shared pool; snapshots saved at iso-loss bands and at token markers 50M / 100M / 200M.

Bias datasets: Multi-CrowS-Pairs (English, 9 categories) + Indian Multilingual Bias (English: caste, gender, religion, race). Metric: PLL only.

Comparative study: the headline is a bias-vs-validation-loss curve — Looped should sit below Vanilla at every matched-loss band, replicated across all 3 sizes. A secondary bias-vs-token-budget plot is reported but is explicitly not the claim.

Statistics: bootstrap 95% CIs always; a paired permutation test on the Vanilla-vs-Looped contrast once ≥ 2 seeds exist. Seeds: 1 to decide, 2–3 to publish.

Green lightLooped clearly below Vanilla at matched loss in ≥ 2 of 3 sizes. Continue; publishable as a short paper.
Red lightVanilla ≈ Looped. Stop the whole program — learned for ~50 GPU-h, not ~510.

Under the hood · Stage 2 specification

New model: ALBERT-Looped (1 unique layer) joins, creating a sharing spectrum: Vanilla (12 unique) → Looped (6) → ALBERT (1). The test becomes whether bias is monotone in sharing degree.

Training data: up to 300–500M tokens; primary size Base-ish plus one smaller scale check.

New metrics & datasets: SS-PLL (surface-form validity control); WinoBias (a structurally different coreference instrument — reports Pro / Anti accuracy and Pro–Anti gap); GLUE (SST-2 + RTE) as a quality screen and bias-vs-utility Pareto.

External calibration (no training): the same pipeline is run on public bert-base-uncased, albert-base-v2, ModernBERT-base to prove it reproduces known bias scores.

First mechanistic teaser (no new training): loop-wise bias trajectory — how preference evolves at each loop boundary (CONVERGENT / AMPLIFYING / OSCILLATING).

Statistics: a small confirmatory family (Vanilla–Looped, Vanilla–ALBERT, Looped–ALBERT) with Holm-Bonferroni correction; everything else labelled exploratory. Seeds: 3–5.

Green lightMonotone (or Vanilla > {Looped, ALBERT}), survives correction, PLL and SS-PLL agree. Continue; stronger paper.
Amber lightOne-metric-only or collapses under correction. Pause and fix measurement first.

Under the hood · Stage 3 specification

New model: Hyperloop-BERT with 4 parallel residual streams mixed by learnable hyper-connections at loop boundaries, plus CWSA (CLS-Weighted Stream Aggregation) — a learned attention over each stream’s [CLS] vector. Compute-matched to Looped.

Primary comparison: Vanilla vs ALBERT vs Looped vs Hyperloop at matched loss, Base-ish, 3–5 seeds, 500M tokens (400M @ seq 128, then 100M @ seq 256).

The causal test: a from-scratch stream-count ablation, n ∈ {1, 2, 4} (300M tokens, 3 seeds). Only stream count changes, so a monotone bias-vs-n trend at matched loss is clean causal evidence; n=1 must collapse to Looped (sanity check).

Full mechanistic suite: loop-wise bias trajectory; CKA representation similarity across loops; stream-disagreement ↔ bias correlation (Pearson + Spearman, labelled correlational); early-merge OOD intervention (merge_at ∈ {1,2,3}); demographic token-drift (cosine drift of group terms across loops).

Extra evaluation: full GLUE (SST-2, MRPC, QNLI, RTE) Pareto; StereoSet in the appendix only.

Statistics: confirmatory family (Vanilla–Hyperloop primary, plus Vanilla–Looped, Looped–Hyperloop, n=4 vs n=1) with Holm-Bonferroni.

Green lightVanilla > Looped ≥ Hyperloop with a clean stream dose-response → full paper. Even Looped ≈ Hyperloop is publishable.
InvestigateOnly if the core effect vanishes at full scale — find the confound before publishing.

Under the hood · the decision rule

Each stage’s analysis script prints a verdict from explicit thresholds on the primary instrument (Multi-CrowS-Pairs, PLL, iso-loss matched). The gating test everywhere is the item-level paired permutation (sentence pairs are the resampling unit), which has real power at 3 seeds; seed-level tests are robustness only. A capability gate runs first: VanillaBERT must show statistically detectable baseline bias (CI above chance) before any contrast is interpreted — an undertrained model cannot be shown to “reduce” stereotypes it never learned. Stage 1: GO / EXTEND-SEEDS / NO-GO / CAPABILITY-FAIL. Stage 2: GO / PAUSE / NO-GO on sharing-spectrum direction + Holm-corrected significance + SS-PLL direction agreement + calibration anchors. Stage 3: GO / PUBLISH-NULL / INVESTIGATE, including the stream-count monotonicity check and the n=1 ≈ Looped sanity check; the stream-disagreement correlation is corroborating only and never licenses the word “causal”.

Table 6 · Statistics and seeds
  • Primary test: item-level paired permutation (10,000 sign-flips) — the resampling unit is the sentence pair (n ≈ 1,500 on Multi-CrowS-Pairs), with per-item effect-size deltas averaged across seeds first. This is what gives real statistical power at 3 seeds: seed-level resampling has a floor of p = 1/2n_seeds (0.125 at 3 seeds) and can never clear 0.05.
  • Seed-level paired permutation retained as a robustness check only — never gates a decision.
  • Phipson–Smyth +1 correction on all permutation p-values (p is never exactly zero).
  • Bootstrap confidence intervals (2000 resamples, 95%) on every reported rate.
  • Holm-Bonferroni applied to the small pre-declared confirmatory family only; everything else labelled exploratory.
  • Cohen’s d for effect sizes; signed Pearson and Spearman for mechanistic correlations (direction matters; correlation never labelled causal).
  • Failure accounting: every unscoreable pair is counted (Failed_Row_Count), tied pairs reported separately (Tied_Pair_Count) — denominators are never silently shrunk.
  • Seeds: 1 to decide go/no-go; 3 (Stage 1) or 3–5 (Stages 2–3) to publish.
Table 7 · Methodology hardening (implementation audit, July 2026)
  • Fixed validation masking: one dedicated RNG seed masks the validation set once; every architecture, size and seed is scored on the identical masked tokens, removing masking noise from the iso-loss control.
  • Iso-loss auditing: fine validation cadence near bands, per-crossing loss registry (iso_checkpoints/index.csv), per-contrast loss-gap tolerance checks.
  • Capability gate: baseline bias on Vanilla must be statistically detectable before any contrast is interpreted.
  • Parameter-matched control: a 6-layer Vanilla (same unique parameter count as Looped) answers the “looped is just a smaller model” objection; exploratory, not confirmatory.
  • Equal-budget stream ablation: n = 1/2/4 all trained at the same token budget — only stream count varies.
  • Early merge as intervention: trained 4-stream weights executed with early merging at eval time (merge_at 1/2/3); out-of-distribution, corroborating only.
  • Decontamination coverage: 8-gram screening plus whole-phrase substring matching for eval sentences shorter than 8 words, and GLUE task text screened out of the training corpus.
  • Capacity disclosure: Hyperloop carries extra hyper-connection parameters vs Looped; disclosed as a limitation, and the stream ablation is conservative with respect to it (more streams = more parameters, working against the predicted direction).
  • Construct scoping: the endpoint is a correlation-type fairness measure in the descriptive / normative / correlation taxonomy of Wang et al. 2025 (Fairness through Difference Awareness, ACL 2025 Best Paper, arXiv:2502.01926). We claim reduced stereotype association at matched quality — not difference-aware fairness. A lower preference rate is not automatically “more fair”: that paper shows difference-unaware treatment is not universally the right target and that bias-mitigation can backfire on difference-aware tasks.
  • Resumability: full mid-run checkpointing (model, optimiser, scheduler, RNG, band tracker, data cursor) every 2000 steps; unattended multi-day runs lose nothing.
